CV rules

What the CV and cover letter buttons follow, and what to apply when you edit by hand.

Never change

  • Job titles, word for word: Backend Engineer, Part-time · Associate II - Software Engineer · Freelance Mobile Application Developer.
  • Employers, dates, degrees, metrics and certification names. They are facts about you, not copy to optimise.
  • Never add a skill you haven't used because a posting asks for it. Name the gap in the cover letter instead.
  • All 5 projects and all 9 certifications stay on every CV. The AI engineering platform always says in progress.

Header and subtitle

  • Line 1: MAYUR DOKRAS. Line 2: Backend Software Engineer | [title from the posting] | 4+ Years Production Experience.
  • Second titles you can use: MLOps Engineer, Platform Engineer, DevOps Engineer, Site Reliability Engineer, Python Developer, Software Development Engineer, Data Platform Engineer, Cloud Engineer, API Engineer.
  • Never use Senior, Lead, Principal, Staff, Architect or Mid-Level, or titles you can't back up (Data Scientist, ML Research Engineer).
  • Location: Pune, India plus "Open to relocation to [country of the role]". Never visa, sponsorship, nationality, date of birth or full address on the CV.

Summary

  • One paragraph, 4–5 lines, rebuilt for each role type: MLOps, Backend or AI.
  • Open with the title and 4+ years, then your strongest proof with a number (around 80% less manual investigation, 40+ defects, 25% lower MTTR).
  • Use the posting's own words for tools ("Kubernetes", not "K8s") and put the ones you really have in bold.
  • No "passionate", "results-driven" or "leveraged", and no list of three vague qualities.

Experience bullets

  • Start with an action verb that shows ownership: Built, Designed, Owned, Led, Delivered, Resolved, Reduced.
  • Verb + the actual system or tool + a number you can defend. Numbers as digits.
  • Put the bullets that match the posting first inside each role; never delete one to save space without saying so.
  • No throughput, latency or "high-scale" claims until you have a real figure to quote.

Skills section

  • Grouped lines in the "Label: items" format. The group that matches the role type goes first (MLOps and Platform for MLOps roles).
  • Inside each line, the posting's tools come first.
  • Snowflake = workshop certificates only; GCP = one certificate. Keep them listed, never build a bullet around them.
  • Not on your CV until you've used them: PyTorch, Spark, Kubeflow, SageMaker, ArgoCD, Django, Celery, Rust, C#.

Writing and format

  • A4, one page, single column, no tables, text boxes, images or header/footer content. Standard section names.
  • No em or en dashes, curly quotes or ellipsis characters; plain hyphens. No Oxford comma in lists.
  • Avoid: spearheaded, leveraged, utilised, delve, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, dynamic, passionate, synergy.
  • Document author set to Mayur Dokras. Read the PDF once before sending.

Before you press Submit

  • The form's screening questions decide most rejections: work authorisation, sponsorship, notice period, salary, relocation. Answer them truthfully and the same way every time.
  • Check the posting is still open on the company's own site, not only on an aggregator.
  • UK roles: the employer must hold a sponsor licence and the salary must meet the Skilled Worker rate for the role.
  • If a role asks for more years than you have, apply only where the posting says years are a guide, and say why in the cover letter.
